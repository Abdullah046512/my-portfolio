Deepfake Audio Detection

Overview

Deepfake audio detection is a binary classification problem: given a short audio clip, decide whether it is a real human recording or a synthetic voice produced by a text-to-speech or voice-cloning system. I built the full pipeline end to end — from loading and cleaning audio to extracting features and evaluating a supervised model.

The project was as much about understanding the data as it was about the model. Audio is noisy, variable in length and easy to preprocess inconsistently, so most of the engineering effort went into a clean, repeatable feature pipeline.

The problem

Modern voice cloning tools make it increasingly easy to produce convincing fake audio. A reliable detector needs to generalise beyond the samples it was trained on, and it must be evaluated in a way that reflects real risk — a fake clip labelled as real is far more costly than the reverse.

Approach

  • Assembled a balanced set of authentic and synthetic audio samples for training and evaluation.
  • Normalised and trimmed clips, then extracted features with Librosa — MFCCs, chroma, spectral contrast, zero-crossing rate and RMS energy.
  • Trained a supervised classifier with scikit-learn and tuned it using cross-validation.
  • Evaluated with accuracy, precision, recall and F1 score, paying close attention to false negatives.

Results

The final model separated authentic and synthetic samples on a held-out evaluation set. I tracked accuracy alongside precision, recall and F1, treating false negatives as the most important error to reduce.

What I learned

  • Feature engineering dominates — how audio is represented matters as much as the classifier itself.
  • Accuracy alone hides class imbalance; precision and recall tell the real story.
  • Consistent preprocessing prevents silent bugs that are hard to trace later.
  • Documenting each experiment made the results reproducible and easier to improve.
